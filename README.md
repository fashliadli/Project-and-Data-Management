## 1. Customer segmentation with RFM

**Problem:** the Yogyakarta sales team had thousands of customers and no clear view of which ones to reward, which to visit and which to give up on.

**What I did:**
- Took the distributor invoices from 2018 to April 2020 and calculated, for each customer, the recency (days since the last invoice), the frequency (number of invoices) and the monetary value (total spend).
- Scored each variable from 1 to 5 in quintiles and mapped the three-digit score by rules to named groups (for example Champions, Loyal, At risk, Lost).
- Defined a treatment for each group, from rewards for the best customers to personal visits and win-back campaigns for large customers who were drifting away.
- Compared the same customers across two periods to spot big customers that were slipping, and mapped the top customers to their sub-districts to show where to focus.
- Presented the analysis to management in the area business review (June 2020).

**Result:** the sales team got a ranked list of customers with an action for each, including large customers at risk that needed a visit first.

**Skills:** customer segmentation, working with invoice data, linking analysis to geography, presenting to management.

## 2. CLUE: one checklist for the field team

**Problem:** the field team visiting modern retail outlets needed five different tools to know which items belong in an outlet, which were already there and how fast they sold. Visits were slow, and sales were lost when an item was missing from the shelf.

**What I did (team of four, with a mentor):**
- Sized the problem: for newly listed items we compared the month a chain listed the item with the month each outlet first ordered it, and valued the gap as potential lost sales.
- Designed one integrated checklist per outlet, maintained by cluster of branches and not shop by shop, with item codes and barcodes taken from the company database instead of typed by hand.
- Made the case for the change with a structured ideation (SCAMPER), a cost-benefit analysis with a measure for each benefit, and an action plan with owners and a timeline.
- Presented the project in the final of the innovation competition IDEAFEST FIND 2023.

**Result:** the integrated checklist now runs nationally.

**Skills:** problem definition from the user's side, quantifying a business problem, solution design, cost-benefit analysis, working with sales, key account management, operations and IT.

## 3. GISEL: gimmick stock monitoring

**Problem:** gimmicks (promotional items) go through distributors and providers and are recorded in several documents and systems. It was hard to see where the stock was and whether the records agreed.

**What I did:**
- Built dashboards in the company BI platform for distributors, one regional area and providers. Stock is calculated as opening stock plus inbound minus outbound, from several internal sources.
- Checked each source against the dashboards and found where the process behind a source was not yet good enough.
- Mapped the reasons for differences between records and agreed adjustment rules for each reason, with a regular recap.
- Ran a trial in one area, then extended it to the other areas.
- Reported progress to stakeholders along the way.

**Result:** the gimmick check now runs nationally.

**Skills:** BI dashboards from multiple sources, reconciling records between systems, process mapping, phased rollout, stakeholder reporting.
